About

Project method and research criteria

This page explains what the project is, why Tehran was selected, how locations were chosen, and how the matching levels work.

What is the project?

Tehran Cinema Atlas

Tehran Cinema Atlas is a digital museum prototype about Iranian films and real places in Tehran. Instead of presenting films only as titles, the website presents locations as cultural objects: each place has a film scene, a current address, visual comparison, evidence, and research notes.

The aim is to show how cinema can preserve urban memory. A street, palace, mansion, university gate, hotel, museum, or square can become part of a film’s atmosphere and part of the viewer’s memory of Tehran.

Why Tehran?

Tehran was selected because it is strongly connected to Iranian cinema, modern urban life, historical architecture, political memory, universities, cultural buildings, and everyday city movement. The city offers a mixture of old and new spaces, from Golestan Palace and Lalehzar to Ferdowsi Square and the University of Tehran.

Selection criteria

  • The location must be a real place in Tehran or a real historical urban area.
  • The project focuses on real locations, not studio sets.
  • The location should have a visible connection to the film scene, architecture, signage, map data, or historical context.
  • Each selected place should add cultural, visual, or narrative value to the film.

Research method

The method is based on comparing film stills with current photographs, map links, addresses, public knowledge about the building or area, and visual clues such as façades, statues, signs, courtyards, gates, and urban layout. Some current photographs may not be taken from exactly the same camera angle as the film frame, so the website separates visual comparison from evidence notes.

Important note

Then vs. Now

Some present-day images may show the same place from a different angle. The project still explains the match through evidence, map links, and visual or historical clues.

Sources

The final version should include image sources, film still sources, map references, articles, books, websites, or museum/location pages used during the research.

Matching levels

How location certainty is explained

Exact Angle

The film frame and current photo can be compared very closely through the same visible sign, façade, entrance, or spatial layout.

Same Place

The location is the same real place, but the current photo may not match the exact camera angle of the film.

Verified Location

The location is supported by map data, address information, sources, or strong visual/historical evidence.

Approximate Area

The exact original object or façade may not survive, but the historical area or urban setting is identifiable.
